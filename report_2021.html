of labelled 2021 bestsellers are written in the present tense — 5 of 22. The 95% confidence interval runs 5% to 40%, which is wide enough that this year alone cannot support a trend claim.

What the 1,135 quotes became

Only event-narration carries tense evidence. Gnomic present (proverbs, aphorisms) is the contaminant this study exists to filter — it is present-tense by nature and appears constantly in past-tense novels.

No evidence of guessing. Every quote in the CSV appears exactly once in the output, tense is set only where the spec allows, and 820 of 844 notes are substantive rather than boilerplate. On a blind draw of 12 labelled quotes I re-read myself, I agree with all 12.

  • They abstained rather than forced. 62 quotes marked unclear, 2 books returned insufficient evidence instead of a label, and future-tense passages were abstained on rather than shoehorned into past or present.
  • They flagged what would flip a label without being able to see the final tally — naming the exact quote and the resulting percentage in each case.
  • They caught the poetry from the text, not the title. Both Gorman collections were identified as verse via surviving line breaks, stanza parallelism and rhyme — then excluded rather than assigned a tense.
  • They reported problems against their own interest, including three gaps in my specification and a dedup failure that let Ukrainian translations through.

Both of my high-confidence failures were present-tense novels I called past. In each case I reasoned from author convention — King writes past third-person, Weir writes retrospective first-person — rather than from any text. That is exactly the failure mode this study was built to avoid, reproduced by me, on the books where it matters most: present tense is the rare class, so a prior that defaults to past is wrong precisely where being right counts.

The agents, reading only quotes, got both. Where my guesses were useful was structural: I predicted the poetry contamination before any classification ran.

What would change these numbers

  • An extractor bug is still baked into this data. My quote cleaner stripped opening dialogue marks — "You wired the kid," Truemann said lost its first mark on 460+ quotes. Stripped dialogue gets promoted to event-narration, and speech skews present. Fixed in code, not yet reflected here. Direction of bias: inflates present.
  • My specification contradicts itself. METHODOLOGY offers "Some stories," she says as an example of event/present, but the precedence rule I added makes that exact string dialogue. An agent caught it. Unresolved, and it recurs.
  • Quotation marks are absent from 87% of quotes, so the quotation-majority rule I made authoritative governs only 13% of cases. Agents fell back on vocatives and register — defensible, but not what the spec says.
  • A past strand hidden inside dialogue. In Project Hail Mary the past Earth thread is conversational, so quotation-majority bucketing hides it from the event count. Counting narration beats inside dialogue would move the book from 96% to ~82% present — into the audit band.
  • Goodreads quotes are selected for quotability, not sampled from the text. That is why 28% of them are gnomic. Whether quotability correlates with tense is still unmeasured, and it is the largest open threat to the design.
